Ask ten people what success looks like, and you'll probably hear ten different answers. Some will point to a billionaire. Others will think of someone surrounded by a loving family. Someone else will admire the person who left everything behind to climb mountains for a living.

The interesting part is that none of them are necessarily wrong.

Success is one of those words we use every day without agreeing on what it actually means.

I think there are three ways we commonly define success.

## 1. Success is wealth

This is probably the dominant definition in modern society.

A successful person is someone who has accumulated money, status, and influence. They're financially independent, respected, and able to live on their own terms.

This view isn't difficult to understand. Wealth buys freedom. It opens doors, creates opportunities, and provides security. It gives people access to experiences, education, healthcare, and influence that would otherwise be unavailable.

In many ways, capitalism has transformed the ancient idea of "survival of the fittest" into something closer to "supervival of the wealthiest". The more resources you control, the more choices you have.

Of course, wealth alone doesn't guarantee fulfillment. History is full of rich people who were deeply unhappy. Still, it would be naïve to pretend that money doesn't solve many of life's problems.

## 2. Success is living a fulfilling life

This is the definition I find most compelling.

Here, success isn't measured by your bank account but by the quality of your life. Are you healthy? Do you have meaningful relationships? Do you wake up looking forward to your day? Are you proud of how you've spent your time?

This definition is universal because it cuts across cultures, careers, and social classes. A teacher, an entrepreneur, an artist, and a retiree can all be equally successful if they have built lives that genuinely satisfy them.

Once you reach that point, there's little left to prove.

## 3. Success is achieving your own goals

The third definition is perhaps the most rational.

Success simply means accomplishing what you set out to do.

If your dream is to become a doctor, success is becoming a doctor. If your dream is to write a novel, success is writing it. If your goal is to raise a loving family, then that is success.

This definition has the advantage of respecting individual values instead of imposing society's standards.

Its weakness is that it assumes everyone has equal opportunities, resources, discipline, and circumstances. Many people never reach their goals—not because they lack ambition, but because life is often unfair.

It is also deeply subjective. Two people can live completely different lives and both be successful according to their own standards.

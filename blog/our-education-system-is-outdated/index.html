Our Education System Is Outdated

Students will spend their lives making financial decisions, interpreting medical advice, voting in elections, evaluating scientific claims, and deciding whom to trust. They’ll be bombarded with statistics, manipulated by advertising, and exposed to misinformation.

Now ask yourself: would you build a school system that looks like the one we have today?

Probably not.

Yet most school systems are still as centuries ago. Subjects have been added around the edges, but the foundation remains the same. These disciplines are valuable, but real question is whether they deserve their current priority.

The Cost of Every Subject

One uncomfortable truth about education is that time is finite.

There are only so many hours in a school day, only so many years before students graduate. Every hour devoted to one subject necessarily comes at the expense of another.

This opportunity cost is almost never discussed.

Recognizing that something has value doesn’t mean it deserves the same amount of time as everything else.

What Are Students Actually Missing?

Many of the mistakes adults make aren’t caused by ignorance of historical facts or an inability to solve quadratic equations.

They’re caused by poor reasoning.

People misunderstand probability and overestimate rare dangers. They confuse correlation with causation. They fall for confirmation bias. They mistake anecdotes for evidence. They misread statistics. They make terrible financial decisions because they never learned basic economics. They misunderstand incentives. They struggle to recognize cognitive biases in themselves.

Ironically, these are the very skills people rely on almost every day.

Knowledge vs. Thinking

Education often emphasizes accumulating information. But information has become cheap.

Today, almost anyone can retrieve historical dates, scientific definitions, or literary summaries within seconds. What remains difficult is evaluating whether information is accurate, relevant, or misleading.

In other words, knowing how to think matters way more than knowing what to remember.

Probability theory, economics, logic, decision science, and cognitive psychology offer exactly those methods. They teach students how to reason under uncertainty, evaluate evidence, and avoid predictable mental errors.

Why Change Is So Difficult

Educational systems are conservative by design. Every established subject has passionate defenders, tradition, departments, and training programs.

Removing even a small portion of any existing curriculum feels like an attack on people themselves, they professions and their culture/passions.

The debate quickly becomes emotional.

Education for the World We Actually Live In

The world has changed dramatically. Artificial intelligence can draft essays. Search engines can retrieve facts instantly. Algorithms influence what we read. Misinformation spreads faster than ever.

Yet many schools continue preparing students for a world where memorization was scarce and information was difficult to access.

Imagine a school system where reasoning itself becomes a core discipline. Where students learn not only history but how historians evaluate evidence. Not only science but statistical inference. Not only mathematics but decision-making under uncertainty.

That would prepare students to make better decisions throughout their lives.
